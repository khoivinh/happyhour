# 2026-08-08 — Sharing Options popover + three-letter city abbreviations

Source brief: `Scratch/2026-08-08-happyhour-sharing-options.md`.

Both shipped. The abbreviation list was built and approved first, in one round, then wired up
alongside the popover it blocks.

- `Scratch/2026-08-08-happyhour-city-abbreviations.md` — the list: 168 cities, 15 explicit refusals.
  Approved unchanged.
- `docs/2026-08-08-sharing-options-plan.md` — the popover plan as agreed, kept as the record of what
  was decided and why. Its §5 describes an earlier, 8-entry version of the abbreviation map that the
  168-city list superseded.

Tests: **97 passing**, up from 92 — two rewritten, five added.

## The real problem the popover solves

`buildShareMessage` (`time-zone-converter.tsx:449-453`) passes the Sidebar's `use24Hour` and
`showZoneAbbr` straight into `buildShareText`. Changing what a recipient sees therefore means
changing your own board, and nothing on screen says so. The Commit Bar's one share control —
`Include Happyhour link` — governs a third thing entirely. The whole fix is three
independently-persisted share prefs; everything else in that plan is chrome.

## Decisions

**Relative time is not a share option.** The brief listed it as a popover toggle, but `buildShareText`
has no concept of it and the tiles compute a relative offset against the hero (local) city
(`digital-clock.tsx:213`) — an anchor a recipient can't see. Three anchors were considered: the
sharer's local city always; the sharer's local city but greyed when it isn't in the share (the
precedent the Sidebar already sets via `relativeTimeDisabled`); and the easternmost shared city.
Khoi dropped it rather than pick one. The popover ships three toggles.

**Abbreviations grew from a footnote to the round.** The first proposal was ~8 cities with
established civilian shorthand (NYC, LA, SF, DC…). Khoi redirected to systematic three-letter codes
across major cities, with one overriding rule: **intuitive beats official.** TOR, not YYZ. But LAX,
SFO and PHL are fine, because those codes already read as their cities.

**Share prefs will be localStorage-only, not cloud-synced.** Adding one to `useCloudSync` touches
five places plus the worker endpoint, and hits the legacy-default hazard at `use-cloud-sync.ts:31-33`
— a cloud record missing the field pushes `false` back and flips the default off for the account.

## Findings that shaped the list

**Population rank cannot select the cities.** Los Angeles is #86, behind Shantou and Zhongshan.
Paris is #180, Toronto #125, Amsterdam #714, Boston #813, Tel Aviv #1274. Any population cut deep
enough to reach Amsterdam pulls in ~700 Chinese and Indian tier-2 cities first. The candidate set is
curated by relevance instead.

**Every row carries its resolved key, because names are ambiguous.** The highest-population "Geneva"
in `cities.json` is Geneva, *Illinois* (#23019) — Genève is `geneve_CH` at #2753. There are five
Washingtons and three San Franciscos. Keys are generated at runtime by `buildLookup`
(`city-lookup.ts:262-273`) with collision suffixes, so the key is the only safe thing to hand the
code later.

**Many cities are stored under local names**, and this changes what "intuitive" means. Zürich,
Genève, Köln, Sevilla, Göteborg, Montréal, Panamá, Ulan Bator, Frankfurt am Main. An abbreviation
has to read against *the label on the tile*, not the English exonym — which is why Köln is refused
(COL derives from "Cologne", a string that never appears on screen, and KOL is Kolkata's) while
Genève keeps GVA.

**Two rules were added beyond the brief**, because the alternative is a worse list:

- **R6 — contested forms go to nobody.** Chennai and Chengdu both want CHE and neither wins, so
  neither is abbreviated. Same for Shenzhen/Shenyang on SHE.
- **R7 — no abbreviation beats a bad one.** Most Chinese and Indian tier-2 cities have no intuitive
  three-letter form for a non-local reader. The code's `?? city.name` fallback makes omission free.

## Verification

A generator and checker live in the session scratchpad (`list.mjs`, `gen.mjs`, `keyindex.mjs`). The
checker asserts every abbreviation is exactly three A–Z characters, that none collide, that no city
appears twice, and that every key resolves against `cities.json` under the real `buildLookup`
algorithm with a matching name. Round 1 passed on the first run: 168 rows, 168 distinct codes.

Worth re-running each round, and worth moving into `scripts/` if the list outlives the iteration.

## What shipped

**`CITY_ABBR` + `shareCityName()`** in `city-lookup.ts`, beside `ZONE_ABBR` and copying its shape and
fallback. Called from the one place city names enter share text (`share-text.ts`). 168 of 30,481
cities; everything else falls through `?? city.name`.

**The Sharing Options popover** (`sharing-options-popover.tsx`), replacing the lone "Include
Happyhour link" checkbox in the Commit Bar with three settings that persist independently of the
Sidebar: 24-Hour Clock (off), Time Zone Name (off), Happyhour Link (on). Note the Time Zone Name
default *differs* from the board's, which ships on — a share should be plain unless asked, whereas a
dashboard you look at all day can afford the label. `buildShareMessage` now reads these instead of
the `use24Hour` / `showZoneAbbr` props, which stay for the tiles.

**`ToggleSwitch` was extracted** from `sidebar.tsx` to `toggle-switch.tsx` rather than copied. The
two surfaces are meant to be visibly the same control; a second hand-rolled switch is how they'd
drift. There is no `ui/switch.tsx` — this is the app's only one.

Implementation notes worth keeping:

- Built on the Radix primitives directly, not `ui/popover.tsx` — that wrapper bakes in `w-72`, its
  own collision padding and zoom/slide animations, and is shared with three `digital-clock.tsx` call
  sites that shouldn't be disturbed.
- The trigger reuses `CommitBarButton variant="secondary"`, which needed `forwardRef` + prop
  spreading so Radix's `asChild` can attach. That's how it gets Copy Link's exact gray by
  construction rather than by copied hex.
- `align="start"` with `alignOffset={-16}` cancels the CommitBar pill's `px-4`, so the popover's left
  edge sits on the *bar's* edge, not the trigger's. Radix still points the tail at the trigger.
- **The reveal animation clips the surface, not the Content.** `clip-path: inset(0 0 0 0)` clips to
  the border box and would have severed the tail; surface and `PopoverArrow` are siblings.
- **The resets had to go.** `enterShareMode` / `cancelShareMode` used to reset `includeLink` to
  `true` on every open. A preference that resets each time you open the bar isn't remembered.
- **Escape needed a guard.** `time-zone-converter.tsx` has a document-level Escape listener that
  tears down share mode; with the popover open, Escape belongs to the popover. Both a
  `stopPropagation` in Radix and a `shareOptionsOpen` check on the listener — same family as the
  time-picker Escape bug guarded by `time-picker.spec.ts`.
- Mobile bar clearance went 132 → 154px: the trigger is a ~44px button where the checkbox was ~22px,
  and only the stacked mobile layout pays for that.

## The design critique pass

A critique agent measured the six screenshots (three themes × desktop/mobile) rather than eyeballing
them, which caught three things worth having:

- **The close X floated 4px high and broke the 20px right gutter.** Both were self-inflicted: a
  `-mt-1 -mr-1` on the button. Removed. Now measured at X-centre == label-centre exactly, and the
  X's right edge flush with the toggle rail beneath it (433px, 20px inset, in every theme).
- **Bottom padding read deeper than the top.** The last row is a 28px box holding an 18px toggle, so
  5px of slack counts toward the optical gap. `pb` 22 → 16, giving a measured 18 above / 21 below.
- **On mobile a fixed 260px popover sliced vertically through a clock row behind it**, which read as
  a clipping bug rather than an overlap. The surface now matches the Commit Bar's own width
  (`calc(100vw-48px)`, the bar's `px-6` inset) — measured 342px against the bar's 342px.

Confirmed correct and left alone: the tail is centred on the trigger to within half a pixel
(278.5 vs 279.0 desktop, 110.5 vs 111.0 mobile), and the left edges align within the 1px shadow ring.

Two of its findings were **not** acted on, as judgment calls rather than defects — see below.

## Still open

- **Live-QA the density on a phone.** `Current time — 9:00 PM JST TOK/2:00 PM CEST PAR/8:00 AM EDT
  NYC` is a lot of initialisms when Time Zone Name is also on. Shipped always-on; if it reads badly,
  the fix is to prefer the full name whenever the zone is shown — one condition in `shareCityName`.
- **The OG card still spells cities out** (`functions/lib/preview.ts`), deliberately: a link-preview
  headline has room, and clarity there beats brevity. Reversing it means generating a copy of the
  map into `functions/lib/`, since `preview.ts:5-9` forbids importing into `client/`.
- **Share prefs are localStorage-only.** Cloud-syncing them is five files plus the worker endpoint.
- The four abbreviations held back — CDMX, Vegas, Philly, NOLA — and the refusal list, both live in
  the Obsidian doc if the call is ever revisited.

Two open design calls, both raised by the critique and both left for Khoi because the brief points
the other way:

- **The un-themed `#333` surface is weakest in *light*, not happy.** The light-theme Commit Bar is
  `#1c1c1e`, so the popover stacks a near-identical dark on a dark 6px away, split by a white slit.
  Happy (`#333` on a white bar on yellow) and dark (`#333` on `#171717`) both read cleanly. The brief
  said explicitly to use the Sidebar's background, and the Sidebar is un-themed — so this ships as
  specified. If it wants changing, light is the only theme that needs it.
- **The "SHARING OPTIONS" header label duplicates the trigger 30px below it.** The brief asked only
  for a close X at the top right; the label was added on top of that. Keeping it for orientation,
  since the popover can be pushed away from its trigger on a small viewport — but cutting it would
  make the panel more compact and is a defensible call.
